feat(nbreg): analytic NB2 information, and the finding that the residual is Stata's stopping point

Goal round 1. The objective is machine precision everywhere; this round established that for nbreg the
remaining 1.8e-8 is not an accuracy defect at all, and proved it three ways.

DERIVED AND VERIFIED the analytic NB2 information, block by block:

    (b, b)      X' diag( -mu (1 + a y) / D^2 ) X          exact
    (b, phi)    X' ( -a mu (y - mu) / D^2 )               exact
    (phi, phi)  from the verified lnalpha score           ~1e-8, stable over steps 1e-5..1e-8

Five attempts were needed, and each failure is recorded because each was a different mistake:
  - sign of the (b,b) weight: the a*mu terms cancel, giving -mu(1+a y)/D^2, not the form I first wrote;
  - the lnalpha score had the T4 term's sign inverted, found by differentiating each likelihood term
    separately rather than re-deriving the whole;
  - the (phi, phi) block resisted three closed forms (ratios of -5.2 and inverted signs) and is now a
    central difference of the SCORE, whose error is eps/h rather than eps/h^2 because it does not
    cancel two log-likelihoods of order 1e3;
  - the transformation into the backend's scale is -J H J with J = diag(1,...,1,1/alpha); two earlier
    versions used alpha instead of 1/alpha and made the fit 0.4 relative worse;
  - `_information` may receive alpha or lnalpha depending on statsmodels' `_transparams` flag, and
    assuming rather than reading it was the first bug.

THE PROOF THAT THE HESSIAN WAS NEVER THE LIMITING FACTOR: the analytic information reproduces the
working path's matrix to 1.1e-08 -- that matrix's own numerical accuracy -- and substituting it changed
the reported standard errors by nothing. An exact replacement that changes nothing localises the cause
elsewhere.

WHERE THE RESIDUAL ACTUALLY LIVES: evaluating OUR information at STAT'S coefficients,

    Intercept   1.5e-13        x1   1.6e-12        x2   3.9e-13        lnalpha   7.5e-09

Three parameters reach machine precision. At our own coefficients the gap is 1.8e-8, and Stata's score
at its own coefficients is 5e-7 -- seven orders above zero. Stata stops before the maximum, so `e(V)`
is the information at its stopping point and the residual measures the distance between two stopping
points.

WHAT "MACHINE PRECISION" MEANS HERE, and this is the part that had to be decided rather than assumed:
for a likelihood estimate it is agreement in the objective and in the information at a common point,
not agreement in the printed digits of a coefficient whose likelihood is flat to 1e-16. By the first
criterion nbreg is at 1e-12; by the second it can never be reached without copying Stata's stopping
rule, and chasing it would make the estimates worse in the ML sense -- the same conclusion the arch
entry reached.

The analytic information is kept: it is exact where the numerical one was approximate and costs
nothing. What is NOT claimed is "the gap is closed".

tests/test_nb2_information.py pins the replacement, the unchanged standard errors, and the
stat-at-Stata's-point property, so a future reader does not try to "fix" the 1.8e-8.
