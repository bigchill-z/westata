fix(xtunitroot): LLC reaches E3 -- three defects, all found by exporting not reasoning

LLC now reproduces Stata to machine precision on two independent fixtures:

    fixture            td          tds         sbar        Var_ep
    30x12 mixed        0.0e+00     1.8e-15     1.1e-16     2.2e-16
    30x15 random walk  5.3e-15     5.8e-15     1.1e-16     0.0e+00

Three defects accounted for the entire gap, which had stood at 1.24 on tds:

1. HAC series. It used only the post-lag slice of the differenced series (10 values) where
   Stata uses the panel's full differenced range (14). That alone put sbar at 0.8259833
   against 0.9624458.

2. tds correction. I scaled by sqrt(N) -- the IPS convention -- but LLC's ``nt`` is r(sum)
   over the per-panel tivar, i.e. the total regression observation count, 390. sqrt(N) made
   the correction term 71x too small and drove tds to -8.01 against -1.77.

3. nu_se. It carried a finite-sample factor sqrt((e(N)-1)/e(N)) that this code path never
   applies. The unfactored value matches Stata's .03298346 to every printed digit.

The method matters more than the fixes, and this entry is the worst offender in the project
on that axis. Twice I built a confident causal story from an INDIRECT measurement and was
wrong:

    "the wvar series differs, mine has less low-frequency variation"
        -> exporting Stata's actual wvar showed a 6.7e-16 match. Wrong.

    "the divisor is wrong, solve for it"
        -> the solved divisor was 17.65, not a clean number, and every whole-number
           candidate failed. The divisor was never the problem.

Each time, exporting the real quantity from a patched copy of _xturllc.ado settled it in
minutes. The source is readable and the values are exportable, so inference should have been
the last resort rather than the first. Two of those wrong turns are now recorded in the
registry as method notes, because the next reader will hit the same temptation.

Also corrected: I removed one of my own tests rather than keeping it green. It asserted
"random walks must not reject"; Stata reports tds = -1.772, p = 0.038, on exactly those
random walks. The assertion encoded my expectation, not Stata's behaviour.
