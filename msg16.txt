docs: record the real sqreg root cause -- Stata re-sorts the dataset, and the permutation depends on the fit

Round 11 corrected the "fweight is not replication" claim. This round found the mechanism behind the whole family of
wrong conclusions in this area, and it is a single fact:

    qreg PERMANENTLY RE-SORTS the dataset.

Measured directly: after any `qreg` call, `y` read back has different values at the same row numbers --

    after qreg:  [1.24422, 3.457174, -0.844048, 1.623444, 1.603277]
    original:    [-0.768042, 1.69279, 1.603277, -0.254503, 0.719906]

Not different numbers: a different order. `qreg_c.ado` line 35 sorts by `r s2` (residuals, then observation
number) before its final LP, and `bsampl_w.ado` sorts by `r w`.

THREE WRONG CONCLUSIONS, ONE CAUSE -- every probe in this area read a column back from Stata after a command that
had permuted the rows, and paired it with a Python frame still in the original order:

    round 5   "qreg fweight is not replication"          sorted w against unsorted y
    round 11  "qreg consumes the RNG"                    the rows moved; the uniform before and after qreg is
                                                        byte-identical, so no randomness was consumed
    round 12  "replicate fits differ on identical data"  they do not: sending the same expanded array to Stata as
                                                        a FRESH dataset, solve_lad matches qreg to 1e-13 on six
                                                        consecutive replicates

WHAT IS NOW EXCLUDED, each by measurement rather than argument:

    RNG                     equal (the uniform is unchanged across qreg)
    draw sequence           weights match Stata element for element, draws 1..4, at n = 20/40/200
    weighted fit            3.5e-16
    LP vertex choice        <= 1e-13 across six replicates
    divisor                 1.64 is not reps/(reps-1) = 1.01

WHAT REMAINS: Stata's sequence when its row order is being permuted BETWEEN replicates. `qreg`'s sort key is
`r s2` -- residuals, then observation number -- so the permutation depends on the previous replicate's FIT. A
stateless implementation cannot mirror that without carrying the permutation through the loop, and the
permutation is itself a function of the fit.

No fix is proposed. The gap stays at 3.9e-02 and the section says why, in those terms.
