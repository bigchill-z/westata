docs(sqreg): both promoted to E3 -- the "bias" was a bootstrap compared against a constant

sqreg and bsqreg are now E3, and the registry entry that has been wrong for four batches is
corrected. It is the most instructive correction in this project because all three of its
claims were false and each was false in a different way.

WHAT THE ENTRY SAID

    "ratios 0.97 / 0.85 / 0.95, systematic downward bias, most likely a different
     replicate-variance convention. Left at E1 rather than claiming agreement."

WHAT THE MEASUREMENT SAYS

    Stata qreg SE(x1)              0.04724609740003598

    seed 20261023   reps 200       0.064280192790   ratio 1.3605
    seed 777        reps 200       0.066030090157   ratio 1.3976
    seed 20261023   reps 2000      0.068220515910   ratio 1.4439
    seed 777        reps 2000      0.068452908368   ratio 1.4489

Stata converges to about 0.0684 and its own two seeds differ by 2.7% at reps(200). My value,
0.068787948208 with ratio 1.4551, sits inside that band.

1. THERE WAS NO BIAS. The earlier figure compared my number against Stata's single reps(200)
   point estimate, ignoring that a bootstrap statistic has its own sampling error -- roughly
   5% per standard error at 200 replicates, larger than the gap I was chasing. The direction
   even flipped between my own two runs (0.85 low once, 1.44 high the next), which was the
   signal that something was wrong with the comparison rather than the estimate.

2. THE VARIANCE FORMULA WAS NEVER IN QUESTION. sqreg.ado does
       mat accum VCE = vl, dev nocons
       mat VCE = VCE*(1/(reps-1))
   which is exactly np.cov(ddof=1). I verified the two spellings agree to 1e-14 on identical
   replicates, and also checked row-replication against frequency weights -- since bsampl_w
   returns weights and qreg_c fits with wvar() -- which agree to 1e-14 as well. All three
   candidate "convention" differences are numerically nil.

3. THE GUESS NAMED A MECHANISM THAT DOES NOT EXIST. "A different replicate-variance
   convention" sounded like a diagnosis and was not one. This is the exact failure mode the
   project keeps fighting: the note told the next reader where to stop looking, and the answer
   was never in the place it pointed.

WHY THIS TOOK SO LONG: I spent the session confirming components that were already correct.
Reading the source first would have shown that `dev nocons` scaled by 1/(reps-1) is the
ordinary covariance, ending the variance theory immediately. The real work was never the
formula -- it was realising that reps(200) cannot adjudicate a 5% question.

RULE NOW RECORDED IN THE ENTRY: to compare a bootstrap against anything, raise reps until the
seed-to-seed spread is under the tolerance, and compare against the converged value. Never
against a single reps(200) run.
