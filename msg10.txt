feat(frontier): implement distribution(exponential) from fron_ex.ado

The gap was `UnsupportedError: frontier: distribution='exponential' is not implemented; only 'halfnormal' is`.
It is now implemented, and the sweep grades it as a measurement rather than a capability gap.

THE LIKELIHOOD, transcribed from `fron_ex.ado` (which states `E_i = V_i - S_COST U_i`,
`U ~ exponential(0, sigma_u)`, `V ~ N(0, sigma_v)`):

    e  = y - x'b
    z  = -S_COST e exp(-lnsig2v/2) - exp((lnsig2v - lnsig2u)/2)
    ll = sum[ -1/2 lnsig2u + 1/2 exp(lnsig2v - lnsig2u) + ln Phi(z) + S_COST e exp(-lnsigu2/2) ]

and the analytic score (g1/g2/g3 in the same file) is transcribed rather than differentiated, which is now the
house rule after two hand-derived second derivatives came out wrong in this session.

VERIFIED TWICE, in the two directions that matter:

  * the likelihood at STAT'S estimates on my own sample: relative difference **0.0** (exactly equal);
  * a full fit against Stata: coefficients 5.95e-08, standard errors 6.13e-08, likelihood 7.07e-16.

The first check is the one that tests the transcription. The second only tests where the optimiser stopped.

STARTING VALUES come from `frontier.ado` lines 347-350, which are specific to this distribution and not the
half-normal split: `sigma2_u = (-S_COST m3 / 2)^(2/3)` from the third central moment of the OLS residuals, with
`sigma2_v = m2 - sigma2_u` floored at 1e-4.

ON THE SWEEP'S OWN DATA THE GRADE IS D, and the reason is recorded rather than smoothed over. There
`sigma_u` is 0.15 against `sigma_v` 1.06, so the surface is nearly flat in `lnsig2u` -- the same region the
half-normal path already documents. Measured: our likelihood is HIGHER than Stata's by 1.13e-06, so we are at
least as close to the maximum, and a refit started AT Stata's point lands between the two, confirming there is
no local trap. Supplying BFGS the analytic gradient (rather than letting it difference the objective) improved
our own gradient norm from 2.41e-05 to 5.72e-06, which is a real improvement to the optimiser but cannot make
`sigma_u` identifiable. The D tier is the surface, not a formula error.

That gradient change moved two pinned values in `test_frontier_observed_information_comes_from_the_analytic_gradient`
by 4e-6 to 1e-6 relative. Both were re-pinned with a docstring note saying they were re-captured and why, so a
future reader can distinguish an intentional refresh from a regression; tolerances are 1e-5 on those two, still
two orders below the 2.3e-3 formula error the test guards.
